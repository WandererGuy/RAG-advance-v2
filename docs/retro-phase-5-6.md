# Retrospective — Phases 5 and 6

**Written** 2026-08-14, closing Phase 5 (API + frontend) and Phase 6 (improvements).
Covers 2026-08-12 → 2026-08-14.

This is a retrospective, not a status report — [`progress.md`](progress.md) says what is true and
[`adr/`](adr/) says why. This file says **what the way of working got right and wrong**, so the next
sprint repeats one and not the other. It is the only document here that is allowed to be an opinion.

---

## The one-paragraph version

Two phases produced three pipelines, two ADRs deciding between them, and the project's first win:
`rerank-v1` improves every retrieval metric over the baseline and now serves `/chat`. The
engineering discipline held — frozen pipelines, committed negative results, one variable per
experiment — and it paid off concretely at least three times. What went wrong is subtler and more
important: **the measuring instrument hit its ceiling before the system did**, and we found out by
accident rather than by watching for it. The binding constraint on this project is no longer the
retrieval code. It is the golden set, and that has been true for longer than we noticed.

---

## What went well

**1. "Run it twice" caught a wrong conclusion in the act.**
`rerank-v1`'s first run scored `refusal_accuracy` 0.600 against the baseline's 1.000 — a 0.4 gap,
well above the ~0.2 noise floor, which looked like a real regression and would have been written
down as one. The second run scored 0.800. The per-question comparison then separated two different
causes that the aggregate had fused: `q029` flipped between runs (sampling), while `q025` failed in
both (reproducible). **One run could not have told those apart**, and the conclusion drawn from it
would have been wrong in a way nothing downstream would have caught.

The rule that forced this (CLAUDE.md 16) was written in Phase 6 experiment 1 after `hybrid-v2` came
back 0.8 then 1.0. It cost 137 seconds and it worked the very next time it was applied. Keep it.

**2. Freezing pipelines made comparison possible instead of merely intended.**
Three pipelines, three immutable results files, three rows generated by `make report` from those
files rather than typed by hand. At no point did anyone have to ask "was that number from before or
after the change?" — the question cannot arise. The cost was real (`hybrid_v2.py` duplicates twenty
lines of `naive_v1.py` because editing the frozen file was not allowed) and it was worth paying.

**3. Committing the negative result was vindicated within two days.**
[ADR-0009](adr/0009-hybrid-retrieval-not-adopted.md) rejected hybrid retrieval and explained *why*:
RRF re-scores a merged list and can push a relevant chunk out of the top 5, which it did to q021.
That diagnosis is what pointed at reranking — an operation that reorders candidates without
dropping any. Experiment 2 was designed directly from experiment 1's failure, and it degraded **0
of 24** questions. A deleted negative result would have taken that reasoning with it.

**4. The registry made adopting a winner a one-line change.**
Switching the served pipeline from `naive-v1` to `rerank-v1` was `PIPELINE_NAME` in `.env`. No code
change, no route touched, no service edited. The Phase 4 decision to resolve pipelines through a
registry rather than importing a class paid for itself here, two phases later.

**5. Provenance survived contact with three pipelines and a provider migration.**
Every results file carries `golden_set_author`, and `leaderboard.md` renders it as a column. Nobody
can quote a number from this project without also being handed the fact that the questions were
agent-written. That was ADR-0004's whole intent and it is still holding.

**6. Documentation stayed a present-tense description of the system.**
CLAUDE.md said `reranker.py` did not exist; when it started existing, the line was rewritten rather
than annotated (rule 14). Two days of work did not leave a trail of "originally / as built"
disclaimers for the next session to reconstruct.

## What did not go well

**1. We measured until the ruler ran out, and only noticed at the end.**
`recall@5` is now **1.000** and MRR **0.979** on 24 questions over 34 chunks. There is no retrieval
headroom left. This did not happen suddenly — Phase 4 already reported 0.958 and
[ADR-0009](adr/0009-hybrid-retrieval-not-adopted.md) explicitly noted "almost no recall headroom to
win", then aimed at MRR instead. That was the right adjustment and it bought exactly one more
experiment.

**The signal was legible a phase earlier than we acted on it.** A dataset where a single question
is worth 0.042 of recall, and where the baseline already scored 0.958, was close to exhausted
before Phase 6 started. Nobody was tracking "how much discriminating power is left" as a number,
so the question never came up until perfect recall made it unavoidable.

**Do differently:** treat headroom as a first-class metric. When the best pipeline is within ~2
questions of ceiling on the primary metric, the next work item is the dataset, not another
pipeline.

**2. `refusal_accuracy` has been "the thing to fix" for three phases and is still not fixed.**
Phase 4 named it. Phase 5 named it. Phase 6 reproduced it deliberately, twice, and then produced a
minimal test case (`q025`). Every entry correctly routed it to a human decision — and then the next
phase started anyway.

The routing was right; the queueing was not. A decision that blocks nothing gets deferred forever,
and this one now has a cost attached: it is the only metric on which the newly-served pipeline
does not beat the baseline. **A blocker with no deadline is not a blocker, it is a note.**

**Do differently:** an item blocked on a human needs an owner and a by-when, or an explicit "we are
shipping without this and here is what that costs" — which is what [ADR-0010](adr/0010-cross-encoder-reranking-adopted.md)
ended up having to write, three phases late.

**3. The corpus was contaminated by demo traffic twice, and the fix is still "remember to run
`make validate`".**
Both times an upload through the Phase 5 UI joined the frozen corpus. Both times `make validate`
caught it. Both times the recovery was manual. The second occurrence was found *at the start of
Phase 6*, meaning an eval run in between would have produced numbers that looked entirely normal
and were measured against the wrong corpus.

`make validate` catching it is good. Nothing *preventing* it is the gap, and "run validate after
any upload session" has now been written in three separate documents — which is what a process
control looks like when it should be a technical one.

**Do differently:** make the eval runner refuse to start against a corpus whose lock does not
match, rather than relying on a human to run the check first.

**4. A frozen module shipped with a latent failure mode we then could not fix.**
With `PIPELINE_NAME=rerank-v1` and an empty `RERANK_API_KEY`, the Voyage path builds cleanly and
raises at the first `/chat` call — LiteLLM is handed `api_key=None`, and only the Jina adapter
validates its key at construction. A deployment that forgets the key starts healthy and fails per
request.

This was found *while writing the ADR that froze the code*, which is the worst possible ordering.
The freeze rule is right; what was missing was a review pass *before* the results file was
committed, while the code was still editable.

**Do differently:** read the new module's failure paths before running the eval that freezes it.
The freeze is triggered by committing results, so the window for cheap fixes closes at a moment
that is easy to walk past without noticing.

**5. An overstated claim nearly entered an ADR as fact.**
The ADR first asserted that `rerank-v1` "cannot start without a key". Checking the code showed that
is true only for Jina. It was caught and corrected before the commit — but it was caught by
verifying rather than by remembering, and the same sentence written five minutes later would have
become a permanent part of the record that a future session would have trusted.

**Do differently:** claims about behaviour in an ADR get checked against the code, not against
recollection of the code. The cost of checking is a grep; the cost of not checking is a document
that lies to whoever reads it next.

**6. Phase 5's demo gate stayed open across two phases.**
Held open for the right reason — an agent cannot sign "a person outside the team used this" — but
"blocked on a human" was allowed to mean "parked indefinitely" rather than "someone is being
asked". Same root cause as item 2.

## For the next sprint

**The single highest-value item is not code.** Rewrite `eval/datasets/golden_qa.v1.jsonl` as a
human-authored `v2`. Every argument for it that ADR-0004 made is still true, and Phase 6 added a
harder one: at `recall@5` 1.000 the dataset can no longer distinguish a good pipeline from a
better one. Experiments 3 and 4 (chunk size, query rewriting) cannot be meaningfully evaluated
until this is done — running them first would produce three more rows that all read 1.000 and prove
nothing.

**Ranked, with reasoning:**

1. **`golden_qa.v2.jsonl`, human-written** — unblocks everything below it. Agent questions
   paraphrase the source text, which flatters dense retrieval and now the reranker sitting on it.
   Human questions quote and abbreviate. ~29 lines, a few hours, and it is the only item here a
   human cannot delegate.
2. **Decide the refusal contract** — three phases old, now with a minimal test case (`q025`) and a
   measurable cost. Either outcome is fine; the deferral is what is not. If hedging becomes
   acceptable, `rerank-v1` scores 1.000 in both runs and sweeps every metric.
3. **Phase 5's demo gate** — one person outside the team, no instructions, ten minutes.
4. **Make contamination structurally impossible** — the eval runner should verify the corpus lock
   itself and refuse to run against a mismatch, instead of documenting that a human should check.
   Two occurrences is a pattern.
5. **`.github/workflows/ci.yml`** — lint + unit tests, ungated, small. `eval.yml` is now unblocked
   too (PLAN.md gates it at ≥3 pipelines and there are 3), but it will burn two metered keys per
   run, so gate it to manual dispatch or a label.
6. **Validate `RERANK_API_KEY` at construction for every provider** — closes the fail-per-request
   trap. It touches frozen code, so it belongs to whoever next opens that path for another reason.

**Deliberately not next:** more retrieval pipelines. `rerank-hybrid-v4` and `chunk-500-v1` are both
one file each and both interesting, and neither can be evaluated against a ceiling. They become
worth doing the day `v2` exists.

**What the two experiments taught, carried forward:** *reordering beats re-scoring.* Hybrid fused
two rankings and could evict a relevant chunk (q021); reranking only reorders candidates dense
already found and evicted nothing (0 of 24 degraded). Prefer operations that cannot lose
information already retrieved.

**No traffic has gone through `rerank-v1` yet.** All 30 rows in `queries` are `naive-v1`, recorded
before the switch. The pipeline is verified against the golden set and by one manual call, not by
use.
